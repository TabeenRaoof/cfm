# Legitimate interests assessments

**Draft, 8 October 2026 (D-059). Answers lawyer question 4 in `README.md` with our view, for the
reviewer to confirm.** Each follows the three-part test: purpose, necessity, balance.

## 1. Invitations, including the account created for the invitee

**Purpose.** A customer organisation adds its own staff. Since self-signup is off (D-057), the
invitee's account is created when they are invited, or they could never sign in to accept (D-058).
The interest is the customer's and ours: a working team product.

**Necessity.** The email address and the role are the minimum that can make an invitation work.
Creating the account up front is necessary only because self-signup is closed; the account holds
nothing but the address until the person accepts.

**Balance.** The invitee is someone their own organisation chose to invite, in a work context, so
being contacted about a work tool is within their reasonable expectations. Safeguards: the
invitation expires after 14 days; an account from an invitation nobody accepted is deleted after
it expires (deletion procedure §D); the address is used for nothing else — no marketing; the
invitee can decline in the app, or ask us to delete everything at once.
**Outcome:** legitimate interests is an appropriate basis. *Open for the reviewer:* whether, for
the invitation itself, the inviting organisation is the controller and we its processor.

## 2. Change history (audit log)

**Purpose.** An accurate, tamper-evident record of who changed an organisation's compliance data
and when. Customers need it to show how their technical file came to be; we need it to
investigate problems and security incidents.

**Necessity.** Recording who changed what is the purpose itself. Keeping before/after copies of
rows (rather than only the fact of a change) goes further than D-005 intended; D-054 accepted it
for richer history while an organisation exists.

**Balance.** The people recorded are users acting in their work role, and people named in the
organisation's own compliance data. Safeguards: readable only by that organisation's owners and
admins; no one can alter or delete entries while the organisation exists; everything is erased
with the organisation (D-054).
**Known limit, disclosed on both legal pages:** a single person's data inside a living
organisation can't be erased from the history. *For the reviewer:* whether that limit is
acceptable at design-partner scale, or per-person redaction must be built before uploads.
**Outcome:** legitimate interests is an appropriate basis, subject to the open point above.
